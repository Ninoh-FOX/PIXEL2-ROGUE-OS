The FC (Family Computer) game console is an 8-bit third-generation home video game console manufactured, published, and sold by Nintendo. The Western version is called the Nintendo Entertainment System (NES), commonly known as the "Gray Machine," and was released in the United States on October 18, 1985. The console's design and game cartridges used in Western regions were completely different from the Japanese Famicom. The Famicom was the best-selling console of its time, with cumulative global sales exceeding 61 million units. The emergence of the FC had a profound impact on the video game industry, helping the U.S. gaming market recover from the 1983 crash and establishing Nintendo's leading position in the modern gaming world.
